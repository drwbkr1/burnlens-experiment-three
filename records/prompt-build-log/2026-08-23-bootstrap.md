## Scientific framing carried into the build

- Primary question: can a deliberately data-sized neural detector avoid the
  canonical U-Net's constant-class collapse on the retrospective Deschutes
  compatibility benchmark?
- Primary architecture direction: biased pointwise convolutions
  `6 -> 8 -> 8 -> 1` with two ReLU activations, totaling 137 parameters.
- Approved bounded protocol direction: event-class-balanced masked BCE;
  deterministic float32 local CPU PyTorch; Adam at `0.001`; batch size `4`;
  maximum `200` epochs with patience `25`; seeds `20260725`, `20260726`, and
  `20260727` with the first primary; no augmentation, positive-class weighting,
  BatchNorm, dropout, ensemble, pretraining, or search; minimum validation
  balanced-BCE checkpoint selection; and one validation-only threshold rule
  prospectively sealed around worst-event Dice and macro IoU.
- Required outcome: a real build, synthetic preflight, training, checkpoint
  selection, safe fresh-process reload, inference, evaluation, packaging, and
  exact replay.
- Comparative outcome: independently recorded as `PASS`, `FAIL`,
  `INCONCLUSIVE`, or `INVALID`; beating RBR is not mandatory.
- Claim boundary: retrospective sparse-core compatibility only, with no
  independent-accuracy, dense-segmentation, significance, generalization,
  superiority, or operational claim.
- Fresh confirmation: separate and owner-gated, never a blocker or post-test
  rescue for the primary experiment.
